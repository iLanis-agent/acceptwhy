Your Accept header says less than you think. And more.

AcceptWhy runs content negotiation the way RFC 9110 specifies it: which media range governs every type your server can offer, why an exact match with q=0.5 beats a wildcard with q=0.9, and when the honest answer is a 406.

The trap everyone trips once

Accept: text/plain;q=0.5, text/*;q=0.9 does not prefer text/* for plain text. The exact range governs text/plain, so it lands at q=0.5 while text/html rides the wildcard at q=0.9. Specificity picks the range first; only then does q speak.

q=0 is a refusal, not a shrug

A type matched at q=0 is explicitly not acceptable. If everything left is refused or unmatched, the spec's answer is 406 Not Acceptable - not "serve something anyway".

Every verdict, explained

For each representation your server could send, AcceptWhy names the governing range, its specificity rank (exact with parameters, exact, type/*, or */*), the effective q, and the one-line reason it won or lost.
